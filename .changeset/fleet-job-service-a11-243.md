The fleet job service (A11-243): fleet work is a job you submit and follow by job ID, not a script in your own shell. `a11y-fleet-jobs.service` is a long-lived process on the control plane that owns a job store (`/var/lib/a11y-fleet-jobs`, files with one writer each) and runs each job's captures against the fleet over the workers' own `/capture`, so a job outlives the agent that submitted it. `fleet capture submit` takes the pages, the boxes (a number, `ready` or names), the capture options of the current scripts, a freeze reference and a label, and returns the job ID at once. `fleet job status [--json]` gives the state, progress per box, an estimated finish and the results' location; `fleet job wait --timeout <duration>` always ends (0 done, 1 failed with the reason, 3 cancelled, 124 timeout with the progress and what is left, 2 usage or unreachable); `fleet job cancel`, `list --label` and `fetch` complete it. Results are one record per page and pass plus a `manifest.json` (pages, boxes with their builds and versions, the pinned versions, start and finish) in a fixed place per job, copied to the agents host by `fetch`. Two jobs never share a box, a box that fails three captures in a row is dropped from its job, and a restart resumes every running job and takes again only the captures the dead process had sent. The CLI reaches the service over the control plane's ssh (a new `controlPlaneInvocation` beside `sshToControlPlane`), so there is no new port. It is put on the plane by `pnpm run fleet:jobs-install`, which runs the service from its own checkout, `/root/fleet-jobs`, and reads the heartbeat back. Nothing on any box changes; the fleet stays frozen.
